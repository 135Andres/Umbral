## 1. THE OBJECTIVE IS ALREADY DECIDED — DO NOT RESTATE IT HERE

`UD-016` fixes the objective: reduce to **O(changes) the reading/hashing of content** during
re-observation, preserving the verdicts, the evidence, the ambiguity and the reconstruction
capability of V0/v0.1 — and **not** claiming O(changes) for the whole filesystem traversal.

**`UD-021` bounds that preservation**, and the bound matters for reading everything below: what
is preserved is the verdicts *within the limits of the evidence each observation actually
holds*. v0.2 does **not** guarantee that an observation which reused prior evidence reproduces
the verdict a full re-read would have produced. The four things that must not be conflated —
verdict equivalence, reuse of prior evidence, content verification, metadata-stable — are
separated in `UD-021`, and the accepted limitation is `F-TD-4` (§3 below).

Those two records are the authority. This proposal does not repeat them; it works out what the
objective implies, and what is still undecided.

The distinction the rest of this document depends on:

```
O(corpus)   traversal / metadata inspection of every entry   v0.2 does NOT optimise this
O(changes)  reading and hashing of content                   the only thing v0.2 promises
```

## 2. THE PROBLEM, STATED PRECISELY

v0.1 re-observes the whole tree on every `observe`, and re-reads and re-hashes every regular
file, whether or not anything changed. v0.1 states this as known limitation 1 — "O(corpus)
per run … not optimised, and not a defect against this version's contract" — so v0.2 is a new
version attacking a declared limitation, **not** a correction of a v0.1 defect.

Measured in v0.1 (`examples/bench.rs`, 2000 files x 4 KiB). **These figures are historical
observations, not a stable benchmark** — see the reproducibility warning below before using
them for anything:

| Metric | Recorded (v0.1) | Share |
|---|---|---|
| scan / metadata walk, **one call** | 23.9 ms | 19% |
| content read + hash, **one call** | 102.5 ms | 81% |
| **one full observation (scan + hash)** | **≈126 ms** | 100% |
| full observation, **two calls** | 209.6 ms | — |

The two-call row is the source metric as `v0.1.md` §EVIDENCE records it: **"full observation
(scan + hash), twice"** measures *two* `observe_set` calls, while `scan` and `hash all files`
are *one-call* figures. The two bases are not interchangeable and are not mixed above.

An earlier revision of this section presented `scan 23.9 ms` and `read + hash 102.5 ms` as
components of the `209.6 ms` total and derived a third component by subtraction
(`remainder (persistence, assembly) ~83 ms (40%)`). **That remainder was never measured.** It
is the arithmetic difference between a two-call total and one-call components, and it does not
correspond to any work in `bench.rs`. It is removed, not corrected: there is nothing to
correct it to.

**Reproducibility warning.** The recorded figures are roughly 2x-3.5x higher than a
re-measurement of the same corpus on the same machine (2026-09-13, five consecutive runs,
tmpfs): scan 7.8-9.4 ms, hash 29.5-54.9 ms, two-call total 74.9-137.4 ms. The spread *within*
one session is already 1.8x, and the recorded run states no repeat count, no system-load
condition and no cache state. The recorded numbers are not wrong; they are **one observation
presented as a baseline**. The corrected arithmetic above is consistent with today's
measurement, which shows `two calls ≈ 2 x (scan + hash)` with no material remainder.

Consequences of the correction: the share v0.2 can attack is **larger** than this section
previously claimed (81% of a per-run observation, not 49%), which strengthens `UD-016`'s
decision to scope the objective to read/hash. `UD-016`'s own text does not contain the
decomposition and needs no correction. **No design decision rests on any of these numbers** —
they are unstable at the 2x level, and the instrument for the objective is the counter
(`UD-016`, and §9 A2-V1), never a stopwatch.

## 3. WHAT v0.2 MUST NOT BREAK

The objective names these explicitly, and they are the reason the optimisation is hard:

1. **Verdicts — bounded, not absolute.** `changes` produces the same verdicts as v0.1 on the
   same fixtures **where the two observations hold the same evidence**. This is now a recorded
   commitment with a bound (`UD-021`), not an absolute requirement, and the bound is a
   consequence of the mechanism rather than a defect in it.

   The accepted limitation (`F-TD-4`, specified in
   [`V0.2-TECHNICAL-DESIGN.md`](V0.2-TECHNICAL-DESIGN.md) §G.3): the skip may retain a previous
   content reading, and a mutation that preserves the metadata the skip predicate uses may go
   **undetected in that observation**. Concretely, a content change that does not move metadata,
   occurring during a run that one implementation read and the other skipped, can surface a run
   later and produce `Modified` where v0.1 reports `Unchanged`.

   This is **not** hidden as a test exception and **not** presented as a guarantee. It is a known
   and reachable limitation of the mechanism. The four things that must stay distinct —
   **verdict equivalence**, **reuse of prior evidence**, **content verification**,
   **metadata-stable** — are separated in `UD-021`.
2. **Evidence.** What the output asserts must stay traceable to what was actually observed.
3. **Ambiguity.** The existing ambiguity classifications are preserved, not resolved by
   picking an answer.
4. **Reconstruction.** The log stays append-only and the state stays reconstructible.

An optimisation that changes any of these fails v0.2 regardless of how fast it is.

## 4. CONTRACT GROWTH — DECIDED IN PRINCIPLE, UNDESIGNED IN DETAIL

`UD-017` decides that the output distinguishes four outcomes and that `basis` expresses
them. It also fixes the rule that makes the whole version legitimate: **if Umbral did not
read the bytes, it may not present the result as content-verified.**

The vocabulary, fixed by that record and not to be changed without justification:

```
bytes read            ->  content-verified
metadata sufficient   ->  metadata-stable / equivalent  ->  NOT content-verified
evidence insufficient ->  UNKNOWN
```

What is **not** yet designed, and is the substance of the implementation work:

- the exact spelling and values of `basis`;
- how `status` exposes the difference without inventing counts nobody can act on;
- the mechanism that makes a `basis` attributable to what it applies to (`UD-019`), including
  how `show` names the entry it reports (`UD-020`);
- the mechanism that makes a `basis` **traceable to the evidence that produced the result** — a
  **separate obligation** from applicability, since the evidence can belong to a different entry
  or to the run itself. Its **semantics are decided** (J.1: *locatable from the result to durable
  evidence*, so the output need not re-enumerate the values); its **mechanism is open**
  (`F-TD-5`; analysed in [`V0.2-TECHNICAL-DESIGN.md`](V0.2-TECHNICAL-DESIGN.md) §E.5–E.11);
- whether the log needs new columns (it will, if ctime enters the condition — §6).

**The mechanism is proposed in [`V0.2-TECHNICAL-DESIGN.md`](V0.2-TECHNICAL-DESIGN.md)** and is
not designed here: the skip decision, the `hash_read_run` attribution, the three `basis` states,
the falsifiable properties and the pre-registered experiments all live in that document. This
section keeps only the scope statement.

Because `basis` is produced by Umbral and not stated by the filesystem, it is **`derived`**.
The `OBSERVED_FIELDS` allowlist and `label_contract_violations` check apply to it unchanged,
and the growth is declared **now**, before the acceptance criteria are fixed — deliberately,
to avoid the v0.1 pattern of discovering a needed distinction after the criteria were frozen
(F-V01-1, F-V01-3).

### 4a. Three design conditions the growth must satisfy (`UD-019`)

`UD-019` refines `UD-017`'s traceability requirement without selecting a representation. The
three conditions below are **conditions on the design of the contract `UD-017` already
introduced** — they are not separate capabilities, and none of them is a feature.

**`V0.2 DESIGN INPUT` — `basis` must have determinable applicability to the entry or object it
describes.** Not "must carry a subject": the requirement is that the relation between a `basis`
and what it applies to is determinable under the contract. The mechanism is open — an explicit
field, a declared convention, or another verifiable representation. Nothing is assumed about
whether it is a string, an independent field, or repeated per line. Evidence: two independent
AI readers could not resolve applicability from positional adjacency alone
(`experiments/exp-ai-01/COMPARISON-AI-01-AI-02.md` §8, §10).

**`V0.2 CONSTRAINT` — `basis` must not introduce an undefined semantic vocabulary.** `F-V01-9`
records that the output's vocabulary is defined nowhere in the output. v0.2 adds a term to that
output; it must not deepen the debt. This does **not** require resolving `F-V01-9` inside v0.2,
and does not reopen it.

**`V0.2 CONSTRAINT` — non-determinability must remain operational, not anthropomorphic.**
`unknown` means a property is not determinable from the available evidence within the contract.
It is not a mental state of the software, and no requirement derived from this work may be
phrased as one.

## 6. THE OPEN QUESTION: DOES ctime ENTER THE SKIP CONDITION?

`UD-018` decides what ctime may be *used to claim* (nothing about content) and explicitly
leaves the mechanism open. This section records the analysis so the decision can be made on
evidence rather than on the appeal of a cheap check. It is **OPEN** — recorded here because
the version record does not exist yet.

**What ctime is.** The inode change time, stamped whenever the inode's metadata changes. It
is **not settable from userland** (Linux kernel documentation, `filesystems/multigrain-ts`):
mtime is settable via `utimensat`, ctime is not.

**Measured locally** (kernel 7.1.13; tmpfs and btrfs; ext4 not available on this machine):

| Case | tmpfs | btrfs |
|---|---|---|
| same size + mtime restored exactly | ctime changed | ctime changed |
| chmod, content untouched | ctime changed | ctime changed |
| hard link created to the file | ctime changed | ctime changed |
| atomic replacement (`rename`) | ino changed + ctime changed | ino changed + ctime changed |
| 300 consecutive writes, ctime collision | 0 / 300 | 0 / 300 |
| minimum observed ctime delta | ~57 µs | ~89 µs |
| `touch -d 2001-01-01` | mtime=2001, ctime=now | — |

The two decisive rows: a writer that restores size and mtime exactly **still moves ctime**,
which closes the specific false negative v0.1 documents as its `stat` guard's blind spot; and
no collision appeared in 600 writes.

**What this does NOT establish.** That ctime always changes. The kernel's own documentation
records that coarse-grained timestamps can make a change invisible within a jiffy, and that
multigrain timestamps — which reduce this — are a per-filesystem opt-in. ctime is Unix-only,
its granularity is filesystem-dependent, and **ext4 was not measured locally**; only CI can
measure it. A filesystem that mirrors timestamps from elsewhere (network, FUSE) is a
different case again.

**The asymmetry that should drive the decision.** A wrong skip corrupts the history
silently; an unnecessary re-read costs time. So the bar for skipping is high, and adding
ctime to the condition can only make it **stricter** — never more permissive. The cost is
accepted: `chmod`, `chown` or a new hard link will force a re-read of an unchanged file.

**The trap to avoid.** "The filesystem changed ctime, therefore the content is the same as
before" is a non-sequitur. ctime is *evidence that something about the inode changed*, not
evidence about bytes. Any mechanism using it must keep the result labelled as not
content-verified (`UD-017`).

**Options, none selected:**

- **(a) size + mtime + dev + ino + ctime.** Closes the known false negative; needs new log
  columns; Unix-only (the field is `Option`, and where absent the skip is disabled).
- **(b) size + mtime + dev + ino.** No schema change; keeps the documented blind spot.
- **(c) size + mtime + dev + ino + ctime, behind a declared conservative mode.** Most
  faithful to the asymmetry, most design cost, most likely to drift in meaning.

**Status, as of 2026-09-13: `ctime` is NOT adopted, and `D-PEND-2` remains `OPEN`.** The owner
considered the recommendation and deferred it. The consequence is stated so it is not
overlooked: the skip condition in v0.2 is **(b)**, and the blind spot it keeps is therefore
**reachable**, not hypothetical — see `L1` and `L2` in
[`V0.2-TECHNICAL-DESIGN.md`](V0.2-TECHNICAL-DESIGN.md) §G.4. Adopting (a) requires `E-TD-2` and
`E-TD-3` (§H of the same document) to have been executed and recorded; neither has been executed.
`ctime` is neither adopted nor eliminated: it remains a candidate technical hypothesis.

## 7. RISKS

- **R1 — the contract erodes in practice.** "Skipped" becomes "verified" by habit. Mitigated
  by `UD-017`'s mandatory rule, by a contractual test that bites (§9), and by asking a
  reader to tell the two apart from the output alone (A2-V13).
- **R2 — a silent false negative.** The blind spot moves from inside the read window to
  between runs. Stated, not hidden. ctime narrows it and does not close it.
- **R3 — optimising the wrong half.** If a real corpus is traversal-dominated (very many
  small files), v0.2 delivers little. This is why the objective is scoped to read/hash and
  why the corpus-dependence is stated (§2) instead of being averaged away.
- **R4 — scope growth by adjacency.** Listing, ctime, watcher and the legibility debts all
  sit next to this work. §5 keeps them out.
- **R5 — a fast benchmark mistaken for a proof.** §9 addresses it directly: the objective is
  demonstrated with counters, never with a stopwatch.

## 8. WHAT THE LEGIBILITY DEBTS HAVE TO DO WITH THIS

F-V01-6 (an `unchanged` whose basis and scope are invisible) and F-V01-7 (a rename whose
justification is invisible) are open v0.1 debts. v0.2 makes the same class of problem worse
before it makes it better: `unchanged` today is one thing, and after v0.2 it must be readable
as *unchanged-by-content* or *unchanged-by-metadata*. If `basis` is added without care, a
reader will be less able to tell what the tool did, not more.

This is the reason §12 of the planning mandate proposed evaluating F-V01-6/F-V01-7 by
`AI-INDEPENDENT` **before** the `basis` contract is frozen. That evaluation is not part of
this proposal's authorization and is not scheduled.

**Two further debts received reinforcement from evidence of a different class.** EXP-AI-01
(`8a50eb2`) produced independent AI reading evidence that bears on `F-V01-8` (where an observed
and a derived value coincide, the labels alone do not let the rule be reconstructed) and
`F-V01-9` (the output's vocabulary is defined nowhere in the output). Both readers identified
`observed` and `derived` and both stated that their meaning is inferred from usage rather than
recoverable from the material.

This changes **nothing** about their disposition. `F-V01-8` and `F-V01-9` remain `OUTSIDE v0.1`
debts and are not reopened, not resolved, and not scheduled by v0.2. What the reinforcement adds
is a second, independent evidence class pointing at the same class of gap — and the reason it is
recorded here rather than in the version record is that it bears on v0.2's design obligation, not
on v0.1's history.

The operative consequence is the `V0.2 CONSTRAINT` in §4a: `basis` must not add a term whose
meaning is not recoverable from the contract. Registering the reinforcement here, and not in
`docs/versions/v0.1.md`, follows the routing rule's "one home per fact": the version record owns
the debts as v0.1 left them, and that statement remains accurate and unedited.

### 9.1 Structure

Two levels, accepted separately:

- **Version criteria (`A2-V*`)** — what v0.2 as a whole must satisfy to be declared evidence
  complete. They state *what*, never *which mechanism*.
- **Slice criteria (`A2-T<n>-*`)** — what one increment must satisfy before the next begins.
  Slices are drafted only when their mechanisms are decided. **Slice 1 is drafted below**; the
  others (`basis` with `show` naming its entry; the O(changes) skip; acquisition history) are
  drafted when M1/M2, the reference representation and the persistence schema are decided.

`Pn` refers to the falsifiable properties of
[`V0.2-TECHNICAL-DESIGN.md`](V0.2-TECHNICAL-DESIGN.md) §G, which supply the falsifiers.

## 10. WHAT EACH CLASS OF EVIDENCE CAN AND CANNOT ESTABLISH

Fixed by `UD-015`; restated here only as it applies to A2-V13 (formerly A2-8), because this is where the
temptation to over-claim is strongest.

| Evidence class | Establishes | Does NOT establish |
|---|---|---|
| HUMAN-INDEPENDENT | legibility for a human reader; A1 | — |
| AI-INDEPENDENT | legibility for a reader with no prior contract knowledge; can surface defects and ambiguities; can unblock technical decisions | legibility for humans; A1 |
| AI-CROSS-CHECK | discrepancies and interpretation problems | authority over another AI's answer |
| AUTHOR | the author's own reading | independence |
| AGENT-INTERNAL | that the tool did what its own record says | legibility; independence |
| automated test | that the distinctions exist and are machine-enforceable | that they are comprehensible |

Consequence: A2-V13 admits **partial** satisfaction from AI-INDEPENDENT during development
(discovering defects, unblocking decisions) and requires HUMAN-INDEPENDENT for its formal
satisfaction. This is the separation the owner approved; it is not a relaxation of A1.

## 11. TESTING STRATEGY

- The v0.1 suite is the regression floor: its tests (114 after the 2026-10-02 corrections) must
  keep passing, on tmpfs, btrfs and ext4. A change to a verdict is a failure, not a test update;
  slice 1 may change only expectations of rendered text (A2-T1-10).
- New tests are written against **hand-built observation sets** wherever the property is
  about classification, so no test depends on what a filesystem happens to do (F-6).
- Filesystem-dependent properties are exercised in the matrix and in CI, and where a
  behaviour cannot be provoked locally it is recorded as not provoked — never asserted.
- Counters are the instrument for A2-V1. A stopwatch may be reported as context; it is never
  the evidence.
- A captured `stdout` is not contractual evidence: if a test depends on a branch having been
  taken, the branch must be observable in the result, not in a `println!` (the v0.1 inode
  reuse probe is the precedent — it printed, and the print was invisible in passing runs).
